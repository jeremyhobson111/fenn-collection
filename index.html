Lot 14

On the night of October 31, 1926, nineteen-year-old Iris Fenn, billed as “The Girl Who Hears the Dead,” sat down at a séance table in her uncle’s Detroit parlor.

Four people joined hands around her. The lamp went out.

When it came back on, her chair was empty.

Her uncle told the newspapers the spirits had taken her. The police closed the file in 1927. She was never found.

It has been one hundred years tonight.

Lot 14 has arrived at your door. It is sealed. Everything you need is in the file.

Statement of Walter H. Pruitt
Age 52. Taken by Det. Sgt. T. Kearney, Nov. 1, 1926, 1:15 a.m.

I am the girl’s uncle and her manager. I took my sister’s girls in when the influenza took her. I have given them everything.

Last night’s sitting was advertised in advance. Five sat at the table: myself, Dr. Moss, Mrs. Crane, Miss Garvey, and my niece Iris as medium. Iris sat before the cabinet, as always.

The Victrola played throughout. Music raises the vibrations. The spirits will not come without it.

My medium wears no jewelry. Metal disturbs the spirits. I have always insisted on it.

My younger niece, June, was asleep upstairs. She is fifteen and takes no part in the work.

The lamp was put out at twenty minutes to ten. The spirits came at once, very strong. When I relit the lamp, Iris was gone.

I know what I witnessed. The spirits took her. I intend to tell the newspapers so.

Statement of Dr. Harlan Moss
Age 47, physician. Taken by Det. Sgt. T. Kearney, Nov. 1, 1926, 1:40 a.m.

I attended as an observer. I have exposed three mediums in this city and fully expected to make it four.

I sat at the medium’s left. When the lamp went out, I took her left hand in my right and I did not let go. Not for one instant. I would swear to that in any court.

Her fingers were cold, and her rings bit into my palm. I remember thinking she gripped very hard for such a slip of a girl.

The knocking began almost at once. Loud, deliberate. It went on for some time.

When the lamp was relit, Mrs. Crane screamed, and in the confusion I let go. I did not see the girl leave. No one could have. I have not slept.

Statement of Mrs. Adelaide Crane
Age 61, widow. Taken by Det. Sgt. T. Kearney, Nov. 1, 1926, 2:05 a.m.

I have sat with Iris every Sunday since my Edward passed. She brought him back to me. I won’t hear a word against her.

I sat at her right hand and held it the whole of the sitting. Her hand was cold, as they are when the spirits are near, and larger than I’d have expected for a girl. I put it down to the spirits.

I wear my wedding band and Edward’s together on the same hand. I have not taken them off since the funeral.

The knocks came from beneath the table, low down, near my knees. They went on well after the lamp went out. Steady. Patient. Like someone trying very hard to be heard.

When the light came back, her chair was empty. I screamed. I am not ashamed of it.

Statement of Miss Nell Garvey
Age 28, typist. Taken by Det. Sgt. T. Kearney, Nov. 1, 1926, 2:30 a.m.

I came because I lost someone. That’s all.

I sat between Mrs. Crane and Mr. Pruitt and held their hands. I saw nothing. It was pitch dark.

Something brushed my ankle once, soon after the lamp went out. Something small. I didn’t cry out.

I didn’t hear anyone leave. I don’t know where Iris is. I hope she’s somewhere safe.

I have nothing else to say.

Detective’s Notes — Det. Sgt. Kearney
Pruitt residence, Alexandrine Ave., Nov. 1, 1926
  • Parlor: round table, cloth to the floor. Medium’s chair placed before the “cabinet,” a curtained alcove at the back of the room.
  • Hall door locked from outside during sittings, per Pruitt’s custom. Key found in Pruitt’s waistcoat.
  • Both parlor windows painted shut. Paint undisturbed.
  • Behind the cabinet curtain: a narrow door to the cellar stairs. Pruitt says it is “never used.”
  • Cellar door to the alley found unbolted from the inside.
  • One set of small footprints in the alley mud, heading east toward the river.
  • Neighbor reports a truck idling in the alley around half past nine. Truck belongs to Daniel Lascoe, 23. Lascoe detained. Says he was “at the river all night, ask anybody.” Known to run liquor across to Windsor. Nobody will vouch for him.
  • Niece, June Fenn, 15. Per Pruitt, asleep upstairs. Bed not slept in. Found in the parlor after the commotion, knees of her stockings gray with dust. Says she came down at the noise. Would say nothing more.
  • Victrola still turning when we arrived, needle in the run-out groove. Record: “I’ll See You in My Dreams.”
  • Miss Garvey’s typing agency could not be located.

Open Questions

Dr. Moss swore he held Iris’s hand all night. Whose hand was he really holding?
The knocking went on after Iris was gone. Who was making it?
Every way out of that room was locked, sealed or watched but one. How did Iris leave?
